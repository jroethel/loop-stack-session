# Brief: extract the board into jrit-board, leave the recording convention behind

Dispositions #52 (closes), #53, #54, #55 (transfer).
Rubix Lens A reviewed 2026-09-11; its findings are folded in, and the sections they changed are noted in Known vs guessed.

## Outcome

The Obsidian board becomes its own private repo, `jroethel/jrit-board`, carrying its own history, its own tracker, and its own config; loop-stack-session sheds every board file and keeps the recording convention that the board reads.

Presupposition verdict: the request framed the loop-stack-session side as "a rollback to before work began on #52".
It cannot be one.
The `(#52)` tag covered two unrelated bodies of work, and the separation is clean enough to compute.
Of the 65 commits tagged `(#52)`, 21 touch board code (`scripts/board*.sh`, `assets/board/`, `tests/board/`) and 5 touch recording-convention code (`session-card.sh`, `take-stock.sh`, `handoff-log.sh`, `lifecycle-lint.sh`, `tests/sessions/`, the two version-coupled templates).
**No commit touches both.**
The remaining 39 are docs, journals, and session records.

The recording convention is what actually answers #52's stated problem, it was rolled to seven other repos on 2026-09-08, and the board consumes it.
So the loop-stack-session side is a forward removal of board paths at HEAD, not a revert; the word "rollback" is retired for the rest of this brief.

The #56 exception needs no special handling.
Its work is commit `96ef2a6` (2026-09-01), tagged `(#56)`, and while it touches four files, none of them is a board file.
A path-scoped board removal preserves it for free.

## End artifact

A private `jroethel/jrit-board` repo that renders Jeremy's cross-repo Obsidian board from a bare `scripts/board.sh`, appears as a card on that board itself, and carries #53, #54 and #55 as its backlog.

## Approach

Chosen: **two repos, with the script-sync obligation accepted and named**.

The honest cost line, corrected after Rubix Lens A: the board vendors and runs its own copies of `tracker.sh`, `take-stock.sh`, and `handoff-log.sh`.
After the split those become forks, refreshed only by a manual, per-file prompted `loop-setup` re-run (`skills/loop-setup/setup.sh:206-225` offers each drifted file and leaves it alone if declined).
So the split costs one extraction, one removal commit, **and a permanent three-script sync obligation** - not the single-commit cost this brief originally claimed.

The split still wins, but on different grounds than first argued:

- The board gets its own tracker, so #53, #54 and #55 stop being filed against a repo that does not contain the board.
- It gets its own release life, independent of B32's packaging work.
- It becomes the home where #55's replacement evaluation happens, and where the board is retired if that evaluation wins - which is the one outcome that makes the sync obligation self-limiting.
- B32 already excluded the board from product v1; the split makes that exclusion structural rather than a filter rule someone must remember.

What it does **not** win: freedom from drift.
The mirror option was rejected at first pass because "two copies drift", and the chosen option has the same defect, relocated from board code to the three vendored scripts.
The difference that justifies the choice is scope: a mirror drifts across the whole board, whereas this drifts across three scripts whose changes are rare and whose contract is now asserted by a test (seam 3).
That is a smaller, bounded, and detectable surface, not an absent one.

Considered: **redesign so the board invokes each scanned repo's own scripts**, making the original "interface not dependency" claim true and removing the sync obligation permanently.
Parked rather than rejected: it is the right long-term shape, but it is a board-behavior change that belongs in jrit-board after the move, not a precondition for it.

Considered: **ship the board as an installable loop-stack skill**.
Rejected because it re-couples the release cycles this split exists to separate and contradicts B32's exclusion of the board from the product.

Considered: **keep the board here and publish a read-only mirror**.
Rejected on scope as argued above, and because the board would keep filing its issues against a tracker that is not its own.

Ordering against B32: **board first**.
B32's seam 1 moves consumer scripts inside their owning skills, and those are the same scripts jrit-board vendors.
Going board-first knowingly buys a second migration - re-pointing jrit-board's vendored scripts once B32 seam 1 lands - in exchange for not blocking the board behind the largest-blast-radius work in either brief.

Dated ecosystem facts this rests on (bought fresh 2026-09-11):
- `git-filter-repo` is present on this host; `git filter-branch` is deprecated and `git subtree split` handles one prefix, so filter-repo is the only clean multi-path option.
- `gh` 2.96.0's active account is `jroethel`, making `gh issue transfer` a same-owner operation for all three issues.
- On this WSL host no repo outside loop-stack-session carries board scripts or a `host.env.template`; the board never propagated anywhere.
