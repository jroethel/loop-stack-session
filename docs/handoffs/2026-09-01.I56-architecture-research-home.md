1. Both exploration steps point inward.
   Brainstorm Step 1 explores files/docs/commits; plan Step 1 explores the codebase.
   No step anywhere obliges an outward look at the current state of the world.
2. The stage penalizes architecture three times and rewards it zero times:
   the Step 3 frontier ban (SKILL.md), the "What the brief is not" delete rule, and self-review scan #3 (both in brief-pipeline.md).
   Sessions learn "engaging with architecture is a cost here" - the exact #52 behavior.
3. Latent contradiction: the Approach section must record "the chosen one, the 2-3 considered, and the rationale",
   but a decision-bearing rationale sentence ("Bases-native kanban plugins now exist") is deletable under a literal reading of the swap test.
   Sessions resolve the conflict in the purity rule's favor because it has a checklist behind it.
4. Answer to the #56 question: which-architecture (candidate shapes, enabling ecosystem facts) lives in brainstorm Step 4,
   recorded in Approach + Known vs guessed; innards of the chosen shape live in loop-plan.
   Not loop-plan for the former - plan consumes the approach decision, it doesn't make it, so research there is only expensive backtracking.
   No new pipeline stage: #52's improvised "tech brief" is the Approach section's scaling behavior, evidence the home is Step 4.

```markdown
## Approaches

Architecture thinking and outward research are required inputs to this step; the purity rules below govern what lands in the brief, never what you investigate.
Before proposing, name each candidate's enabling assumptions about the outside world - ecosystem, tooling, platform capabilities - and buy a fresh observation for any that is decision-bearing and possibly stale.

Propose 2-3 genuinely different approaches with trade-offs, lead with your recommendation, and say why.
The chosen approach, the alternatives, the rationale, and the dated ecosystem facts it rests on all go in the brief; decisions without recorded rationale get relitigated.
```
